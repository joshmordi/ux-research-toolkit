# Writing Task Scenarios

A task scenario tells the participant what to do without telling them how to do it. If the scenario names the button, the menu, or the page, it is leading and the result is worthless.

## Rules

1. Describe the goal, not the steps. Say what the participant wants, not where to click.
2. Give a realistic reason. People try harder when the task feels real.
3. One task at a time. Hand over the next scenario only when the current one is done.
4. Do not help. If they get stuck, note it. That is the finding.

## Bad vs good

Bad: "Click the Account menu, then select Order History to find your last order."
Good: "You bought a pair of shoes last month and want to check whether they have shipped yet."

Bad: "Use the search bar to look for laptop stands."
Good: "Your neck hurts from looking down at your laptop all day. Find something on this site that would help."

## For each task, record

- Starting point (usually the home page)
- Success: what the participant must reach or complete
- What to measure: success or failure, time, wrong turns, questions asked
